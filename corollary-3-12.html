Conventions of this document

Every item below separates three things:

  • Paper: what the text says, with page and step.
  • Needs: what a formalization would have to prove, stated as a proposition.
  • Assessment: a judgment by the author of this document. It is labeled as such, and it is not a claim about whether the proof is correct.

The kind of justification the text gives is recorded as one of the following:

  • argument: a derivation from earlier statements.
  • citation: a reference to another result.
  • assertion: a claim with no derivation, such as "one verifies immediately" or "follows formally".

Gap categories. Gaps are classified with the following seven categories:

  1. failure of proof search;
  2. missing library theorem;
  3. gap in the formalization;
  4. insufficient hypotheses;
  5. counterexample to an intermediate claim;
  6. counterexample to the faithfully formalized original statement;
  7. demonstrated contradiction.

Why categories 5 to 7 do not occur. None of the objects of the corollary is formalized in this project, so no counterexample or contradiction can have been demonstrated. Every gap below is therefore category 2, 3 or 4; category 2 occurs only as a possibility not yet checked (question 3).

  • Category 3 means the vocabulary or the step is missing from the formalization.
  • Category 4 means the explicit hypotheses in the text do not visibly suffice, and a formalization would have to add a named hypothesis.

Category 4 here is a provisional classification of the text as written; it is not a demonstrated insufficiency.

Notation used here

The symbols below are a minimal formal vocabulary chosen for this document. They are not the paper's notation, and they are not defined in Lean.

  • Region spaces.
    • For j ∈ |F_l| and v_Q ∈ V_Q, U^Q_{j,v_Q} denotes the paper's 1,◦U^Q_{j,v_Q} = I^Q(S^±_{j+1}; 1,◦D^⊢_{v_Q}) (page 174).
    • A region is a family X = {X_{j,v_Q}} of subsets.
  • Averaged log-volume. μ̄(X) = (1/l*) Σ_{j∈F*_l} Σ_{v_Q} μ_{j,v_Q}(X_{j,v_Q}) is the procession-normalized log-volume (Prop 3.9 (i), (ii), pages 115 to 116).
    • The index set of the average is F*_l = {1, ..., l*}: pages 173 and 181 say explicitly that "the average is taken over j ∈ F*_l". The factor 1/l* is not displayed; it is what an average over F*_l means. Pages 102 (the capsules of Prop 3.4 (ii) carry the labels j ∈ {1, ..., l*}), 116 (average over "the various capsules under consideration") and 120 (Rem 3.9.3, j ∈ {1, ..., l*}) corroborate this; page 116 alone does not exclude the 1-capsule of label 0 in the procession of page 153.
    • Regions are indexed by j ∈ |F_l|. The formula above adopts the reading in which the j = 0 component does not enter the average; the text does not state this rule (question 4).
    • The inner sum over v_Q ∈ V_Q is an infinite sum. Prop 3.9 (iii), page 117, defines the global log-volume only on families whose local log-volume is zero for all but finitely many v_Q.
  • Indeterminacy group. G is the group generated by the actions of (Ind1) and (Ind2) on the region spaces (Thm 3.11 (i), page 154).
  • Ind3 relation. X ⊑ Y is the relation "Y is obtained from X by the upper semi-compatibility containments" (Thm 3.11 (ii), page 156; Prop 3.5 (ii)).
  • Transport. τ is the identification 0,◦U ≅ 1,◦U induced by the étale-picture permutation symmetry (Step (xi-b), page 181).
  • The Θ-side quantity.
    • R_Θ is the region obtained from the Θ-pilot object at (0,0) via the Kummer isomorphisms of Thm 3.11 (ii) on the 0-column, transported by τ.
    • 𝒰 = ⋃ { Y : ∃ g ∈ G, g·R_Θ ⊑ Y } is the union of possible images (page 174).
    • Ū = hull(𝒰), the holomorphic hull of Remark 3.9.5 (i).
    • −|log(Θ)| = μ̄(Ū).
  • The q-side quantity. Q is the region representing the q-pilot object at (1,0) on 1,◦U^Q (Step (xi-d), page 183), and −|log(q)| = μ̄(Q).

Assessment. The definition of 𝒰 is one reading of "union of possible images ... subject to (Ind1), (Ind2), (Ind3)". The text does not give a formula; see question 7.

The statement and its numerical layer

Paper (pages 173 to 174).

  • Definitions.
    • −|log(Θ)| ∈ R ∪ {+∞} is the procession-normalized mono-analytic log-volume of the holomorphic hull of the union of possible images of a Θ-pilot object, subject to (Ind1), (Ind2), (Ind3).
    • −|log(q)| ∈ R is the log-volume of the image of a q-pilot object, not subject to the indeterminacies.
    • |log(q)| > 0.
  • Conclusion. Two conjuncts: (F) −|log(Θ)| ∈ R, and (I) −|log(Θ)| ≥ −|log(q)|. The "i.e." clause on page 174 reads: C_Θ ≥ −1 for any real C_Θ with −|log(Θ)| ≤ C_Θ·|log(q)|.
  • The "i.e." clause reformulates (I) only. When −|log(Θ)| = +∞, no real C_Θ satisfies its premise, so it holds vacuously while (F) fails. A faithful statement is therefore (F) ∧ (I), or equivalently (F) ∧ (the "i.e." clause); in neither formulation may (F) be dropped.

Already checked in Lean. IUT/PaperIII/Corollary312/Numerical.lean checks the following, given the two numbers:

  • the conclusion implies the "i.e." clause (constantForm_of_conclusion); the "i.e." clause holds when −|log(Θ)| = +∞ (constantForm_of_top); under the separate hypothesis that −|log(Θ)| is finite, the two forms are equivalent (conclusion_iff_constantForm);
  • the opening reduction to −|log(Θ)| < 0 (inequality_of_nonneg: a nonnegative value already satisfies (I)); the reduction concerns (I) only, and finiteness (F) remains a separate obligation, argued on page 175 by compactness (C7);
  • the fact that Step (xi-f)'s passage from the inclusion −|log(q)| ∈ R_{≤−|log(Θ)|} to the inequality is Iff.rfl.

That file builds with lake env lean IUT/PaperIII/Corollary312/Numerical.lean, and no error was found in it. It does not construct either number.

Thirteen questions about the passage from Theorem 3.11 to Corollary 3.12

1. Are the q-pilot and Θ-pilot objects comparable?

Paper.

  • Step (xi-c), page 182: the hull is needed to express output data as localizations of arithmetic vector bundles, "necessary in order to render the output data in a form that is comparable" to the q-pilot object, which arises from an arithmetic line bundle.
  • Step (xi-d), page 183: comparability is achieved only by passing to a suitable positive tensor power of the determinant and then applying the normalized log-volume, giving "completely comparable objects" R_{≤−|log(Θ)|} and −|log(q)|.
  • Step (xii), pages 185 to 186: global realified Frobenioids are needed, because local Frobenioid objects are known only up to isomorphism class, which leaves a p_v^N multiple undetermined.

Needs. Comparison happens in three places, and each needs its own statement:

  • (a) Both Ū and Q are regions in the same space 1,◦U^Q (C1 below).
  • (b) For a hull H (a region λ·O), the degree of det_{⊗M}(H) equals μ̄(H) times an explicit normalization constant, and Q is treated with the same constant, so the tensor power cancels (C4). Pages 132 and 133 state this for hulls, with the details left to the reader.
  • (c) The comparison is made in R, after log-volumes are taken.

Kind. Argument for the need to pass to determinants; assertion for the claim that the result is comparable.

Assessment. The objects become comparable only as real numbers. As regions, Ū is a rank-r object and Q a rank-1 object. A formalization must therefore state precisely which real number is attached to each, with one fixed normalization.

Gap. Category 3: no vocabulary for hulls as vector bundles. Category 4 for (b): the effect of the tensor power M on log-volume is not written as a formula (Rem 3.9.5 (vii), (Ob4); page 183).

2. Are the comparison maps valid?

Paper.

  • Step (xi-b), page 181: the identification 0,◦U ≅ 1,◦U "arises from the permutation symmetries discussed in the final portion of Theorem 3.11, (i)".
  • Thm 3.11 (i), pages 154 to 155: these symmetries induce poly-isomorphisms Prc(n,◦D^⊢_T) ≅ Prc(n',◦D^⊢_T) and n,◦R^LGP ≅ n',◦R^LGP, compatible with the bi-coricity poly-isomorphisms n,◦D^⊢_0 ≅ n',◦D^⊢_0.
  • Step (xi-a), page 181: the Θ^{×μ}_LGP-link is "interpreted as a sort of gluing isomorphism" so that the Θ-pilot at (0,0) corresponds to the q-pilot at (1,0).

Needs.

  • (a) τ is a well-defined map of region spaces. If it is a poly-isomorphism, the induced map on regions is well defined only modulo G, and this must be stated.
  • (b) τ preserves μ̄ (C1).
  • (c) The "gluing" correspondence of (xi-a) is the value group portion of the link (Step (i), page 175), which acts on F^{⊩▶}-prime-strips. A formalization must show the Θ-pilot is carried to the q-pilot by that portion (Remark 3.8.1, page 114).

Kind.

  • (a): citation of Thm 3.11 (i), whose proof is an assertion ("follow immediately from the definitions", page 159).
  • (b): citation of Prop 3.9 (ii), proved by assertion.
  • (c): argument by definition.

Assessment. Two maps are used in different roles:

  • the link, a value group isomorphism carrying Θ-pilot to q-pilot;
  • τ, a permutation-symmetry identification of mono-analytic containers.

The proof uses the first to relate the pilot objects, and the second to place the Θ-side region in the 1-column. The text does not state a single commutative diagram containing both.

Gap. Category 3.

3. Are the volume measures compatible?

Paper.

  • Prop 3.9 (i), pages 115 to 116, fixes the normalizations:
    • integral structures have log-volume 0;
    • multiplication by p_v adds −log(p_v) at nonarchimedean places;
    • multiplication by e adds 1 at archimedean places;
    • the result is invariant under permutations of A.
  • Prop 3.9 (ii), pages 116 to 117: mono-analytic log-volumes are compatible with these via the natural poly-isomorphisms of Prop 3.2 (i), after "adjusting appropriately" (Rem 3.9.1).
  • Prop 3.9 (iv), page 117: log-volumes are compatible along each column.
  • Thm 3.11 (ii)(a), page 155: the Kummer isomorphisms are compatible with log-volumes; page 156: "precisely" compatible along the column.

Needs.

  • (a) Every isomorphism in each poly-isomorphism of Prop 3.2 (i) preserves μ (Ism-invariance at nonarchimedean places; invariance under independent ±1 at archimedean places).
  • (b) The p-adic logarithm preserves measure on the compact sets used (Rem 3.9.4 (iii), page 124 states μ_k(S) = μ_k(log_k(S))).
  • (c) The global log-volume equals the arithmetic degree (Prop 3.9 (iii), page 117).

Kind. Prop 3.9 as a whole: assertion ("follow immediately from the definitions"). Rem 3.9.4: explanatory argument.

Assessment.

  • (a) and (b) are standard facts about Haar measure once the spaces are fixed, so they are candidates for Mathlib-level proofs.
  • (c) carries the phrase "relative to a suitable normalization", which is not specified.

Gap.

  • Category 2 for (a), (b): measure theory on tensor products of local fields and the change-of-variables statement for the p-adic logarithm may be missing from Mathlib; this has not been checked.
  • Category 4 for (c).

4. Are normalization factors preserved?

Paper.

  • Averaging index. The statement (page 173) and Step (x) (page 181) say explicitly that the average is taken over j ∈ F*_l, hence with factor 1/l* (the factor itself is not displayed). Corroboration: Prop 3.9 (i) (page 116) averages over the capsules of a procession as in Prop 3.4 (ii), and Prop 3.4 (ii) (page 102) and Rem 3.9.3 (page 120) use the capsules labeled j ∈ {1, ..., l*}, identified with F*_l.
  • Indexing of objects, not of the average. Thm 3.11 (i)(a) indexes the local packets by j ∈ |F_l| (page 153), and the proof of Cor 3.12 indexes the sets U_{j,v_Q}, U^Q_{j,v_Q} and the hulls by j ∈ |F_l| and v_Q ∈ V_Q (page 174). These pages state no normalization factor.
  • Page 183. Step (xi-d) applies the log-volume "[suitably normalized, with respect to j ∈ |F_l|]". No factor is displayed. This is the one place where |F_l| is attached to the normalization.
  • Label 0. Page 174 recalls that the symbol △ is the result of identifying the labels 0 and ⟨F*_l⟩; page 181 cites the same identification as a source of the averages over F*_l. Rem 3.3.2 (page 101, text) identifies the zero label of |F_l|-processions with the diagonal label.
  • Rem 3.9.3 (page 120) argues the weights must be equal.
  • Step (x) (page 181) says tensor products identify multiplication by integers at different labels.
  • Prop 3.9 (iii) has "a suitable normalization".
  • Rem 3.9.1 notes a factor of 2 at archimedean places.
  • Rem 3.9.5 (vii), (Ob4) introduces an M-th tensor power.

Needs.

  • (a) One averaging rule (index set F*_l, factor 1/l*, and a fixed treatment of the j = 0 component), used for both −|log(Θ)| and −|log(q)|.
  • (b) The q-pilot region Q is measured with the same rule and weights as Ū.
  • (c) The factor M of the determinant power and the archimedean factor 2 cancel or are applied identically on both sides (C4).

Kind. Assertion.

Assessment.

  • The averaging index is not ambiguous in the statement: it is F*_l, factor 1/l*. The sets on pages 153 and 174 are indexed over |F_l| because the procession contains the capsule of label 0, not because the average runs over |F_l|.
  • What is not stated is how the j = 0 component of a region indexed over |F_l| enters a log-volume averaged over F*_l: it may be dropped, or identified with the diagonal label ⟨F*_l⟩ as on pages 174 and 181. Page 183's "with respect to j ∈ |F_l|" does not settle this. An average over |F_l| would carry the factor 1/(l*+1) instead; nothing on the five pages displays that factor. A formalization must fix this rule and use it on both sides.

Gap. Category 4 (normalization not fixed in the text).

5. Are choices independent or correlated?

Paper.

  • (Ind2), page 154: independent copies of Ism (or ±1) act on each direct summand.
  • Remark 3.9.5 (vii), (Ob5): independence of the possibilities is handled via bounded families φ(P_B).
  • Step (xi-b), page 182: the algorithm is treated as "some" algorithm, temporarily forgetting its internal structure (HIS).

Needs.

  • (a) The union 𝒰 is taken over the group G generated by the actions of Ind1 and Ind2, with the (Ind2) choices at different direct summands independent (page 154). Page 154 does not say that the two actions commute, so G is not assumed to be a direct product.
  • (b) The q-pilot region Q is fixed, a single choice not subject to G (page 174).
  • (c) The comparison of Q with Ū does not require a choice in 𝒰 correlated with Q.

Kind. Ind2 independence: definition. Statement (c) is not addressed.

Assessment. Under the reading in this document, Ū is a hull of a union over all independent choices, so −|log(Θ)| is a sup-type quantity. The conclusion then asks that the fixed value μ̄(Q) be at most this sup. Whether some particular choice relates to Q, or the sup alone suffices, is the content of C5 versus C6 below.

Gap. Category 3.

6. What is the ambient structure of the hull?

Paper.

  • Rem 3.9.5 (i), page 127:
    • the hull is the smallest λ·O containing U, with every component of λ nonzero, when U is relatively compact;
    • otherwise it is all of I^Q(−);
    • it is defined only when U contains a relatively compact subset of finite log-volume.
  • Page 175: hulls are taken relative to the holomorphic structure labeled "n,◦".
  • Step (xi-c), page 182: the hull 1,◦Ū ⊇ 1,◦U is taken in the 1-column.

Needs.

  • (a) The hull is taken in 1,◦U^Q with the ring structure labeled 1,◦, after transport by τ.
  • (b) Existence and uniqueness of the smallest λ·O (componentwise).
  • (c) The hull map is monotone, inflationary and idempotent on direct product regions. Remark 3.9.5 (ii) says these properties characterize it uniquely.

Kind.

  • (b): assertion ("one verifies immediately that the holomorphic hull is well-defined").
  • (c): argument.

Checked in Lean. IUT/PaperIII/Remark3_9_5/HullOperator.lean proves, for arbitrary families of sets 𝓗 ⊆ 𝓟:

  • (P1), (P2), (P3) alone force φ(P) = ⋂{H ∈ 𝓗 | P ⊆ H};
  • hence at most one such map exists;
  • closure of 𝓗 under these intersections gives existence.

Assessment.

  • The text gives closure under intersections as the reason for the intersection formula. The Lean check shows the formula follows without it, and that closure is what gives existence.
  • The hull of a union 𝒰 that is not a direct product region needs the definition of (i), not that of (ii).
  • Transporting a region from the 0-column structure to the 1-column structure and then taking the hull is a choice of which holomorphic structure governs the hull. The text states this choice (page 175, page 182) but does not argue that the hull is independent of it.

Gap. Category 3 for (a), (b). Item (c) is done in the abstract.

7. Are the indeterminacies simultaneous and consistent?

Paper.

  • Ind1 and Ind2 are defined in Thm 3.11 (i), page 154; Ind3 in Thm 3.11 (ii), page 156.
  • Thm 3.11 (iii)(c), (d) (page 158) say constructions are compatible "up to the indeterminacies (Ind1), (Ind2), (Ind3)".
  • Step (x), page 181:
    • the indeterminacies are regarded as data describing a "formal quotient";
    • log-volumes are invariant under Ind1 and Ind2 "[one verifies immediately]";
    • log-volumes convert Ind3 into an inequality from above.
  • Rem 3.11.4 (page 173) adds "invisible" indeterminacies (roots of unity on theta values; ±1 on copies of F_mod^×).

Needs.

  • (a) A single definition of the set of possible images as an orbit-and-closure under the joint action. This document uses 𝒰 = ⋃{Y : ∃ g ∈ G, g·R_Θ ⊑ Y}.
  • (b) μ̄ is invariant under G as a group (C2), not only under its generators separately.
  • (c) The Ind3 relation is compatible with the G-action: X ⊑ Y implies gX ⊑ gY, so the union is well defined independent of the order of application (C3).

Kind. Assertion for (b). (a) and (c) are not addressed.

Assessment.

  • Ind3 is described as an indeterminacy but defined as a semi-compatibility (containment) property.
  • Ind1 is defined as automorphisms of the procession (page 154), but in Step (x) it is said to arise from permutation symmetries of the étale picture (page 181). The text does not show the two descriptions coincide.

Gap. Category 4 for (a) and (c): the set of possible images is not defined by a formula. Category 3 for (b).

8. Is the argument circular?

Paper.

  • Step (xi-e), page 184: SHE "amounts precisely to" expressibility via operations that are well defined even under the condition that the input pilot-object log-volume equals −|log(q)|.
  • Step (xi-f), page 184: "the construction of the subset R_{≤−|log(Θ)|} ... is subject to the condition that this construction ... constitutes ... a construction [perhaps only up to some sort of 'approximation'] ... of the pilot-object log-volume of the input data". "The inclusion ... hence also the inequality ... then follows formally."

Needs. A proposition P about the algorithm, its input and its output with the following two properties:

  • P is proved from the properties of the multiradial algorithm, namely IPL and SHE.
  • P implies μ̄(Q) ∈ R_{≤ μ̄(Ū)}.

Kind. Assertion followed by a formal step.

Assessment.

  • At the level of real numbers, the inclusion and the inequality are the same proposition. Numerical.lean proves stepXIf_inclusion_iff_inequality by Iff.rfl. So the "formal" step adds nothing.
  • Everything rests on establishing the inclusion from IPL and SHE. If P is formulated only at the level of the two numbers, it coincides with the conclusion.
  • A non-circular formalization must formulate P at the level of regions or of the algorithm; see C5 and C8.
  • This is a statement about what a formalization must contain. It is not a statement that the proof is circular.

Gap. Category 4: the hypothesis that gives the inclusion is not named as a proposition in the text.

9. Is there an exchange of ∃ and ∀?

Paper.

  • −|log(Θ)| is the log-volume of the hull of the union over all possible images (∀ choices enter the union).
  • The conclusion compares it with a single value.
  • Step (xi-b) speaks of "a collection of possibilities of output data" and Step (xi-c) of possibilities "linked/related" to the q-pilot.

Needs. The inequality compares μ̄(Q) with the log-volume of the hull of the union of the possible images (pages 173, 174, 183, 184), not with the hull of any single image. Two sufficient routes:

  • (∃ form, C5 or C5') there exist g ∈ G and Y with g·R_Θ ⊑ Y such that Q ⊆ hull(Y), or at least μ̄(Q) ≤ μ̄(hull(Y));
  • (∀ form) for every admissible choice, μ̄(Q) ≤ μ̄(hull(Y)).

Each suffices for the conclusion, given hull(Y) ⊆ Ū and monotonicity. Neither is necessary. Counterexample in the abstraction (identity hull, finite measure): let the possible images be the singletons {a} and {b}, each point of measure 1/4, and let Q = {a, b}. Then log μ(Q) = log(1/2) = log μ({a} ∪ {b}), so Q is dominated by the union, while each single hull has log-volume log(1/4) < log(1/2). The supremum of the single-image hull log-volumes is attained here and still lies below μ̄(Q). The obligation that the text sets up is the union-level comparison μ̄(Q) ≤ μ̄(Ū); it remains open. This comparison is the inequality conjunct (I) itself: it is what has to be proved, not a hypothesis that may be adopted (compare C6).

Kind. Not addressed explicitly. "linked/related ... via isomorphisms of F^{⊩▶}-prime-strips" (pages 182 to 183) is a relation between each possibility and the input, which reads as a ∀ statement about IPL.

Assessment.

  • IPL, read as "every possible output is related to the input by an isomorphism of F^{⊩▶}-prime-strips", is a ∀ statement about a relation.
  • What the inequality needs is a statement about the log-volume of the hull of the union. An ∃ statement about a single possibility (some possibility has hull log-volume at least μ̄(Q)) is one sufficient route.
  • Passing from IPL to that route requires that the F^{⊩▶} relation preserve the pilot-object log-volume between Q and some region whose hull lies in Ū. That is C5. A route through the union itself, with no single dominating possibility, is not excluded by the text.

Gap. Category 4.

10. Is compatibility separate or joint?

Paper.

  • Step (iii), page 176: unit and value group portions must be related "simultaneously" through a single link; using two links makes this "practically impossible".
  • Step (xi-b), page 182: the F^{⊩▶} portion of the relation consists of full poly-isomorphisms, while the F^{⊢×μ} portion is "somewhat more complicated" because of Ind1, Ind2, Ind3.
  • Remark 3.11.1 (vi), (vii), pages 164 and 165 (rendered):
    • (vi): the naive tautological algorithm "λ-th power of q" has SHE, at the cost of sacrificing the analogue of IPL (page 164).
    • (vii), distinct labels: IPL holds; for SHE the diagram reads "??": it is "by no means clear" how to establish it (pages 164 to 165).
    • (vii), forced identification: SHE holds; for IPL the diagram reads "??" (page 165). Considering both assignments simultaneously and consistently then forces q to be well defined only up to q^{λ^n}, n ∈ Z, a trivial multiradial representation {q^{λ^n}}_{n∈Z} which satisfies the formal analogues of both IPL and SHE but is useless for the log-volume estimates of Cor 3.12 (pages 165, (viii)).

Needs.

  • (a) A single relation between input and output that respects both the value group portion (IPL) and the unit portion (SHE) at once (C8).

Candidate missing compatibility conditions

These are propositions that a formalization would have to prove, or adopt as named hypotheses, to pass from Theorem 3.11 to the inequality. They use the notation section above. For each, the vocabulary it needs is listed, followed by a comparison with the conclusion μ̄(Q) ≤ μ̄(Ū).

All the verdicts below are assessments by the author of this document.

C1. Transport preserves log-volume.

  • In words: the permutation-symmetry identification τ of the 0,◦ and 1,◦ region spaces preserves the procession-normalized log-volume.
  • In symbols: μ̄(τ X) = μ̄(X) for all regions X.
  • Vocabulary: processions, étale-picture permutation symmetries, tensor packets, log-volumes.
  • Verdict: weaker than the conclusion and independent of it. It concerns only the Θ side.

C2. Joint invariance under Ind1 and Ind2.

  • In words: the averaged log-volume is invariant under every element of the group generated by Ind1 and Ind2, acting jointly.
  • In symbols: μ̄(g X) = μ̄(X) for all g ∈ G and all X.
  • Vocabulary: Aut(Prc), Ism, the tensor packet decomposition.
  • Verdict: weaker; independent of the q side.

C3. Ind3 is monotone and G-stable.

  • In words: Ind3 relates a region only to regions containing it, and commutes with G.
  • In symbols: X ⊑ Y ⇒ X ⊆ Y and X ⊑ Y ⇒ gX ⊑ gY. Hence μ̄(X) ≤ μ̄(Y) by monotonicity of measure.
  • Vocabulary: log-Kummer correspondence, Prop 3.5 (ii) containments.
  • Verdict: weaker.

C4. Common normalization.

  • In words: the q-pilot region and the hull are measured by one and the same averaged log-volume, with one averaging rule. The determinant power M multiplies the log-volume of both sides by the same factor, or is divided out identically.
  • What page 132 states (Rem 3.9.5 (vii), (Ob3-1), (Ob3-2), (Ob3)): the "determinant" det_{⊗M}(−) is applied to the arithmetic vector bundle defined by a hull (Ob2, page 131). It is formed from suitable positive tensor powers of the determinant line bundles of the direct summands, the exponents corresponding to the weights (the products of the N_v of Rem 3.1.1 (iv)), and then tensored with the inverse of the determinant of the structure sheaf O_(−) (Ob3-1-2). M is the positive integer such that det_{⊗M} sends an arithmetic line bundle to its M-th tensor power. "We leave the routine details to the reader."
  • What page 133 states (Ob3-3): via Prop 3.9 (iii), the arithmetic degree of the output may be interpreted, "by working with suitable normalization factors", as the log-volume of the original arithmetic vector bundle, and the step may be omitted when one only computes log-volumes.
  • In symbols, a candidate form: for H in the class of hulls λ·O (direct product regions that are hulls, Rem 3.9.5 (i), (ii)), deg(det_{⊗M} H) = c_M·μ̄(H) with an explicit normalization constant c_M (for line bundles c_M = M), and −|log(q)| = μ̄(Q) with the same averaging rule. The pages justify this only for hulls, through the weights of Rem 3.1.1 (iv) on direct product regions; no identity is stated for arbitrary regions, and c_M is not displayed.
  • Vocabulary: arithmetic vector bundles over O_{K_v}, determinants, Prop 3.9 (iii) normalization.
  • Verdict: weaker. It is needed for the conclusion to be well posed.

C5. Region-level containment (IPL and SHE combined).

  • In words: some admissible possible image has hull containing the q-pilot region.
  • In symbols: ∃ g ∈ G, ∃ Y, g·R_Θ ⊑ Y ∧ Q ⊆ hull(Y).
  • Vocabulary: all of the above, plus IPL and SHE as properties of the algorithm.
  • Verdict: a sufficient route, strictly stronger than the inequality conjunct. With C3, C4 and the monotonicity of hulls it implies μ̄(Q) ≤ μ̄(Ū). The converse fails in general: equal log-volumes do not give containment, and the union can dominate Q when no single image does (question 9). Of the conditions listed here, this is the closest region-level reading of (xi-c) to (xi-f).

C5'. Log-volume version of C5.

  • In symbols: ∃ g ∈ G, ∃ Y, g·R_Θ ⊑ Y ∧ μ̄(Q) ≤ μ̄(hull(Y)).
  • Verdict: a sufficient route for the inequality conjunct, given hull(Y) ⊆ Ū and monotonicity, and weaker than C5. It is not necessary: the hull of a union can have larger log-volume than the hull of every member, even when the supremum over members is attained (example in question 9). The comparison the text requires is at the level of the union, μ̄(Q) ≤ μ̄(Ū), and remains open.

C6. Number-level membership.

  • In symbols: −|log(q)| ∈ R_{≤−|log(Θ)|}.
  • Verdict: a restatement of the inequality conjunct (I) only, by Iff.rfl in Numerical.lean. It says nothing about finiteness: with −|log(Θ)| = +∞ the set R_{≤−|log(Θ)|} is all of R and C6 holds. The full conclusion is C6 together with (F) −|log(Θ)| ∈ R. Adopting C6 as a hypothesis would be a vacuity trick in the sense of the trust policy in CONTRIBUTING.md.

C7. Finiteness.

  • In words: (a) the union of possible images is relatively compact in each U^Q_{j,v_Q} (the compactness asserted on page 175), so each local hull is a proper λ·O with finite local log-volume (Rem 3.9.5 (i), page 127); and (b) the local log-volumes of the hulls are zero for all but finitely many v_Q (Prop 3.9 (iii), page 117), or at least summable, so that the averaged global log-volume is a real number.
  • Vocabulary: compactness of Ism-orbits; boundedness of the Ind3 containments; the support condition of Prop 3.9 (iii).
  • Verdict: independent of the inequality. Parts (a) and (b) together give the separate first conjunct −|log(Θ)| ∈ R; (a) alone does not, since V_Q is infinite.

C8. SHE condition does not shrink the output.

  • In words: imposing on the input the condition "pilot-object log-volume equals −|log(q)|" (Step (xi-e)) leaves the collection of possible outputs, and hence Ū, unchanged, and the q-pilot region remains related by IPL to some output.
  • In symbols: for the restricted input class I_q = {inputs with μ̄(pilot) = −|log(q)|}, 𝒰(I_q) = 𝒰 and C5 holds for some input in I_q.
  • Vocabulary: a formal notion of the algorithm's input class and of "expressible relative to the 1-column structure".
  • Verdict: its first clause 𝒰(I_q) = 𝒰 is an auxiliary condition, weaker than the conclusion and independent of it. Its second clause is C5 restricted to the input class I_q, so C8 as a whole implies C5 and is at least as strong.
  • Status as a reading: C8 is a stronger candidate reading, not what the text states. Page 184 states SHE as expressibility "via operations that are valid/executable/well-defined even when subject to the condition" that the input pilot-object log-volume equals −|log(q)|. Rem 3.12.2 (d^toy) to (f^toy), pages 190 and 191, describes constructing the assignment with indeterminacies "simultaneously with/without invalidating" the conditions of the other assignment. Neither page asserts equality of output sets 𝒰(I_q) = 𝒰.

Summary of verdicts.

  • C1 to C4 and C7 are weaker auxiliary conditions. C7 (with its support condition) is the finiteness conjunct.
  • C5 and C5' are sufficient routes to the inequality conjunct, not necessary ones. The union-level comparison μ̄(Q) ≤ μ̄(Ū) is the open obligation.
  • C6 is a restatement of the inequality conjunct only.
  • C8 contains C5 and adds the condition that restricting the input does not change the output. It is a stronger candidate reading of (xi-e), not a statement of the text.

The text's Step (xi-f) phrase "is subject to the condition that this construction ... constitutes ... a construction of the pilot-object log-volume of the input data" can be read as C6, as C5', as C5 with C8, or as a comparison made directly at the level of the union. Which is intended is the central interpretive question for a formalization.

Lean statement
theorem step_viii_prime_count_prm :
  ∃ η : ℝ, 2 ≤ η ∧ ∀ x sle : ℝ, 1 < x → 0 ≤ sle → sle ≤ Nat.primeCounting ⌊x⌋₊ →
    Real.log x * sle ≤ 4 / 3 * (x + η)
